How Job Hunter works

Job Hunter looks through company career sites and LinkedIn for software jobs in the countries you pick (the Netherlands by default), throws away what doesn't fit (too senior, wrong role, pays too little, outside your countries), ranks the rest against your skills, and gives you one list to work through. Everything runs on this machine and costs nothing.

The 60-second tour

  1. Settings → Profile: add your name, email, pitch and skills. Skills decide how jobs score; the rest feeds your motivation letters.
  2. Companies: employers searched directly. Add or remove any; Test checks it works.
  3. Jobs (the dashboard): press Scrape now, then work down the list, best match first. It also scrapes by itself on a schedule ({% if cfg.schedule.enabled %}every company once every {{ cfg.schedule.every_hours }} hours, one at a time in the background, at low CPU priority{% else %}currently off{% endif %}; change it).
  4. Open a job's Details, copy the motivation letter, press Open application page, apply, then press I applied so it moves to the Applied tab.

Quiet in the background

Built to share a small computer with other things: every company is refreshed once every {{ cfg.schedule.every_hours }} hours, one at a time, at low priority. Only new job postings are processed: a posting that's already in your list (or was already rejected) is skipped. A role a company re-opens for a new batch counts as new (new ID, or posted more than {{ cfg.search.repost_gap_days }} days after the saved one). To make the app look at a job again, open it (or the Hidden tab) and press 🗑 Delete. Hide (✕) dismisses a job for good. When you change rules in Settings, jobs you already have are re-scored right away.

The score (0–100)

Points for: a junior or medior title (+15), skills from your profile found in the posting (up to +25, more for skills in the title), pay (stated +15, typical +15, assumed +10), a fresh posting (up to +10) and a low experience requirement (+5). Unknown pay at an unlisted company costs −10. Open any job to see exactly why it got its score.

How salaries are calculated

Postings quote pay in many ways, so everything is converted to a yearly gross figure before it is compared with your minimum: a monthly salary becomes monthly × 12, plus {{ cfg.search.vacation_allowance_pct }}% holiday pay (Dutch vakantiegeld, only for jobs in the Netherlands, and not when the posting says it is already included), a stated bonus or 13th month is added (one-off sign-on or relocation money never is, and nothing is added when no bonus is stated), and other currencies are converted with the rates in Settings → Advanced. A figure with no period (for example "€4.500 – €6.000") is read as monthly. Hover over any pay label to see the exact calculation.

Several countries

Tick the countries to search in Settings → Matching (now: {% for c in picked_countries %}{{ c.flag }} {{ c.name }}{{ ', ' if not loop.last }}{% endfor %}). With more than one, the Jobs page gets a Country filter. Each ticked country gets its own row in the pay table (minimum pay and minimum for big companies, in that country's own currency, e.g. £70,000 for the UK), prefilled with your current minimum converted, and pay is shown in each job's local currency. LinkedIn is searched once per country.

Pay: what the labels mean

Most postings don't state a salary, so the app works down this list until it finds something:

€95k–120kStated in the posting. If it's below your minimum ({{ min_label }}) the job is dropped, unless it's a big company (below).
≈ €105k–125k typicalTypical pay for that company and level, from the built-in reported-pay table, otherwise from a quick web search of Glassdoor / Levels.fyi / Indeed snippets. Counts as fine when the middle of the range reaches your minimum. These figures are mostly total compensation (with bonus and stock) and self-reported, so base pay is lower.
🌱 big co, under {{ min_label }}Big-company exception. You said you'd accept a bit less at a company with career potential. Companies marked Tier 1 may pay down to {{ big_label }} (any job title; each country has its own pair of numbers in Settings → Matching). Change this in Settings → Matching.
💰 likely {{ min_label }}+ (assumed)No data found, but it's a Tier 1 company, so it's assumed to pay well. A guess.
⚠ typically €50k–65kData says it pays too little. Hidden by default, not deleted.
pay unknownNo data and not a Tier 1 company. Hidden by default (Pay → Everything shows them).

Motivation letters

Every job page has a letter draft built from your own facts (Settings → Profile: your ambition and concrete achievements): it quotes a real line from the posting, uses the two achievements that match the job best, names only skills your achievements actually show, states honestly a skill the job wants that you don't have yet, ties your ambition to the role, and leaves one [bracketed] sentence for you: why this company. It is written in the posting's language (English or Dutch). ↻ Another angle re-words it; Copy prompt for an AI assistant gives you a ready prompt to paste into any free AI chat for a polished version. No tool can promise to pass recruiters' or software's AI screening; specifics and your own words are the strongest signal, so always fill the bracketed sentence and adjust a phrase or two.

AI agent and your CV (optional)

Upload your CV in Settings → Profile (PDF, Word, txt or md; the text is read and stays editable), then switch on the agent in Settings → AI agent with a Claude or OpenAI-compatible key, or the address of a model running on your own network. Every job page then gets ✨ Agent Customize: it sends the posting, your profile, achievements and CV (plus anything you type in the box) to the AI service you chose in the settings (Claude, an OpenAI-compatible service, or a model on your own network) and puts only the letter in the letter box for you to check; the built-in draft is one click away. Nothing is sent unless you press the button. Hosted services cost a few cents per letter on your own account, so set a spending limit on the key (a local model is free). The key is write-only: it is never shown again.

Applying

The app does not submit applications for you: forms differ per company, sit behind logins and captchas, and auto-submitting could send the wrong thing in your name. It makes applying fast instead: Apply ↗ opens the company's own page; each job page has a motivation-letter draft with a copy button, a notes box and status buttons; My snippets has one-click copy for your details. Statuses: new → starred → applied → interview → rejected, or hidden to dismiss.

Where the jobs come from

  • Public job feeds (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, SmartRecruiters, Workday, Jibe, Amazon, Uber): fast and reliable.
  • A headless browser for sites with no feed (Google, Meta, Apple, Microsoft): it opens the search page like a person and reads the results. {% if browser_ok %}Installed on this machine ✓{% else %}Not installed on this machine: run pip install -r requirements-browser.txt && playwright install chromium (or sudo apt install chromium and set the browser program in Settings → Advanced), then restart the app.{% endif %}
  • LinkedIn: its public search, to find employers that aren't on your Companies list. Low volume, never uses your account. LinkedIn's terms forbid scraping, so it can be turned off in Settings → LinkedIn.

The same job found twice (e.g. on LinkedIn and the company's site) is merged, and the company's own link is kept.

Which settings do what

ProfileYour details, ambition, achievements, CV, skills & weights.
MatchingCountries to search, minimum pay per country (and the lower big-company minimum), max years of experience, age, remote.
LinkedInOn/off, search terms, experience levels, how far back and how many pages.
Pay lookupsWeb search for typical pay, plus your own pay data per company.
ScheduleHow often it scrapes by itself.
AdvancedTitle rules (regular expressions) and browser options.
CompaniesAdd, edit, pause, test and remove employers.

Changes are saved in the app's database and apply immediately. Only the network address/port and file locations live in config.yaml (changing the port from this page could lock you out), and each settings page has a reset-to-defaults button.

If something looks wrong

  • No jobs at all → Status: did the scrape run, did sources fail? A company showing failed usually needs its details corrected on the Companies page (use Test).
  • Too few jobs → try Pay → Everything, lower the minimum pay, or raise the max years of experience.
  • Too many irrelevant jobs → raise the minimum score filter, trim your skills list, or edit the title rules in Advanced.
  • Pay looks wrong → numbers are estimates; the job's detail page says where each one came from. Add your own known figures in Pay lookups.
  • LinkedIn finds nothing / fails → LinkedIn sometimes blocks automated access for a while. Everything else keeps working.
