How Job Hunter works

Job Hunter looks through company career sites and LinkedIn for software jobs in the Netherlands, throws away what doesn't fit (too senior, wrong role, pays too little, outside NL), ranks the rest against your skills, and gives you one list to work through. Everything runs on this machine and costs nothing.

The 60-second tour

  1. Settings → Profile: add your name, email, pitch and skills. Skills decide how jobs score; the rest fills the cover letter.
  2. Companies: employers searched directly. Add or remove any; Test checks it works.
  3. Jobs (the dashboard): press Scrape now, then work down the list, best match first. It also scrapes by itself on a schedule ({% if cfg.schedule.enabled %}every {{ cfg.schedule.every_hours }} hours{% else %}currently off{% endif %}; change it).
  4. Open a job's Details, copy the cover letter, press Open application page, apply, then press I applied so it moves to the Applied tab.

The score (0–100)

Points for: a junior or medior title (+15), skills from your profile found in the posting (up to +25, more for skills in the title), pay (stated +15, typical +15, assumed +10), a fresh posting (up to +10) and a low experience requirement (+5). Unknown pay at an unlisted company costs −10. Open any job to see exactly why it got its score.

Pay: what the labels mean

Most postings don't state a salary, so the app works down this list until it finds something:

€95k–120kStated in the posting. If it's below your minimum (€{{ cfg.search.min_salary }}) the job is dropped, unless it's a big company (below).
≈ €105k–125k typicalTypical pay for that company and level, from the built-in reported-pay table, otherwise from a quick web search of Glassdoor / Levels.fyi / Indeed snippets. Counts as fine when the middle of the range reaches your minimum. These figures are mostly total compensation (with bonus and stock) and self-reported, so base pay is lower.
🌱 big co, under {{ cfg.search.min_salary // 1000 }}kBig-company exception. You said you'd accept a bit less at a company with career potential. Companies marked Tier 1 may pay down to €{{ cfg.search.big_company_min_salary }} (any job title). Change this in Settings → Matching.
💰 likely {{ cfg.search.min_salary // 1000 }}k+ (assumed)No data found, but it's a Tier 1 company, so it's assumed to pay well. A guess.
⚠ typically €50k–65kData says it pays too little. Hidden by default, not deleted.
pay unknownNo data and not a Tier 1 company. Hidden by default (Pay → Everything shows them).

Applying

The app does not submit applications for you: forms differ per company, sit behind logins and captchas, and auto-submitting could send the wrong thing in your name. It makes applying fast instead: Apply ↗ opens the company's own page; each job page has a ready cover-letter draft with a copy button, a notes box and status buttons; My snippets has one-click copy for your details. Statuses: new → starred → applied → interview → rejected, or hidden to dismiss.

Where the jobs come from

  • Public job feeds (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, SmartRecruiters, Workday, Jibe, Amazon, Uber): fast and reliable.
  • A headless browser for sites with no feed (Google, Meta, Apple, Microsoft): it opens the search page like a person and reads the results. {% if browser_ok %}Installed on this machine ✓{% else %}Not installed on this machine: run pip install -r requirements-browser.txt && playwright install chromium (or sudo apt install chromium and set the browser program in Settings → Advanced), then restart the app.{% endif %}
  • LinkedIn: its public search, to find employers that aren't on your Companies list. Low volume, never uses your account. LinkedIn's terms forbid scraping, so it can be turned off in Settings → LinkedIn.

The same job found twice (e.g. on LinkedIn and the company's site) is merged, and the company's own link is kept.

Which settings do what

ProfileYour details, cover-letter template, skills & weights.
MatchingMinimum salary, big-company exception, max years of experience, age, remote, what counts as "in the Netherlands".
LinkedInOn/off, search terms, experience levels, how far back and how many pages.
Pay lookupsWeb search for typical pay, plus your own pay data per company.
ScheduleHow often it scrapes by itself.
AdvancedTitle rules (regular expressions) and browser options.
CompaniesAdd, edit, pause, test and remove employers.

Changes are saved in the app's database and apply immediately. Only the network address/port and file locations live in config.yaml (changing the port from this page could lock you out), and each settings page has a reset-to-defaults button.

If something looks wrong

  • No jobs at all → Status: did the scrape run, did sources fail? A company showing failed usually needs its details corrected on the Companies page (use Test).
  • Too few jobs → try Pay → Everything, lower the minimum salary, or raise the max years of experience.
  • Too many irrelevant jobs → raise the minimum score filter, trim your skills list, or edit the title rules in Advanced.
  • Pay looks wrong → numbers are estimates; the job's detail page says where each one came from. Add your own known figures in Pay lookups.
  • LinkedIn finds nothing / fails → LinkedIn sometimes blocks automated access for a while. Everything else keeps working.
